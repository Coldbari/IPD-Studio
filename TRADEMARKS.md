## What is fine without asking

- **Calling it what it is.** Saying that your work uses, integrates with, was
  built with, or is compatible with IPD Studio. Referring to it by name in
  documentation, articles, talks, papers, issues and comparisons.
- **Forking under the license.** The noncommercial license permits forks. Keep
  [LICENSE](LICENSE) and the `Required Notice:` line intact, as the license
  itself requires.
- **Screenshots and links** in reviews, tutorials and coursework.

## What to avoid

- Naming your own product, service, company or domain in a way that suggests it
  **is** IPD Studio, or that it is an official version, edition or successor.
- Presenting a fork as endorsed by, affiliated with, or maintained by this
  project when it is not.
- Using the name or icons as the branding of a commercial offering — that is a
  question for a commercial agreement, not something the license settles.

If a fork diverges enough to have its own identity, giving it its own name is
the clearest outcome for everyone, and there is no objection to it saying what
it was forked from.
